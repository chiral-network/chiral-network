## Why

The author adopted a target design in which a provider no longer needs a public HTTPS origin: a provider behind NAT or carrier-grade NAT serves over libp2p, reached directly, by hole punching, or through a relayed, end-to-end-encrypted circuit (`docs/chiral-book.md`, Part I *Direction, precedence, and status*, and Part II *Peer-to-peer provider transport (target design)*). The book makes this milestone 3 of the desktop local-client plan and says the wire change is "specified in OpenSpec before implementation". Today the offer requires a public HTTPS `endpoint`, the inference router is HTTP-only, every node forces Kademlia server mode, and nodes run the relay server behaviour with near-default circuit limits and no relay client, DCUtR or AutoNAT. This change specifies the transport, the offer schema change and the relay rules, so that sharing from a home desktop (milestone 4) has a contract to build on.

## What Changes

- Mount the provider's existing public router on a second listener: the libp2p stream protocol `/chiral/inference/http/1`, carrying HTTP/1.1 and SSE over Noise and yamux. The router is built once and shared by both listeners, so rate windows, arrival envelopes, slots and the cancellation registry are one state, and status and cancel reach an operation whichever listener started it. Administration, engine, tokenization and metrics routes are never on it. The stream server enforces stream caps, a head deadline, a maximum head size, an idle timeout and a maximum number of requests per stream before the router's own limits.
- Consumers authenticate the provider by the offer's signed `peer_id` (the Noise-authenticated remote peer) before writing any request bytes. They refuse a mismatched peer and never fall back to an address or relay that cannot prove the peer ID. The stream client follows no redirects, never dials an address named in a response, surfaces a 3xx without `Location`, and keeps the HTTPS client's deadlines and 8 MiB response bound.
- A connectivity ladder: direct, then DCUtR hole punching, then a circuit relay v2 circuit. Every node runs AutoNAT and uses Kademlia server mode only with a confirmed public address; AutoNAT dial-backs go only to the requester's observed public IP. Providers hold relay reservations, without needing an offer, and publish their direct and relayed addresses. Addresses learned from peers are filtered with the existing public-address predicate.
- Any peer may hold a coordination-sized reservation (libp2p's 120-second, 128 KiB defaults). Serving relays apply explicit, operator-chosen serving-grade limits, quotas and rate limits only to circuits whose destination currently has a valid signed libp2p offer from its own authenticated manifest, or had one that expired less than 24 hours ago, counted from the signed `expires_at` of the most recent qualifying offer the relay itself verified for that peer and compared on the relay's wall clock with no skew allowance and bounded by a monotonic cap. Per the author's 2026-11-16 decision, this grace lets a provider that drains, stops sharing, pauses publication during a chain outage or is performance-withdrawn keep serving request status, receipts, cancellation and existing accounts through relays, within the same per-provider quotas. The relay keeps one durable evidence record per peer in a protected, network-scoped store under its data directory, reloaded on start, so a relay restart does not end the grace; a missing, unreadable, corrupt or insecure store fails closed for grace and is reported in authenticated administration status. The records are bounded by an explicit operator-configured relay-wide quota whose evictions (earliest grace end first) are reported and are the only way the relay ends a grace early apart from a forward clock step or a failed store; the pre-funding disclosure says a relay whose evidence store is full can end the grace early. Limits cover the whole circuit. Consumers dispatch a paid request only on a circuit whose remaining budget can carry it, and serving streams never ride coordination-sized circuits. Relays forward only ciphertext and have no protocol authority. Per the author's 2026-11-16 relay-operation decision, relay operation adds no fee, payment, agreement or term. Shipped profiles may name project-operated serving relays, and providers may list relays that any operator runs. A project relay's serving-grade circuits carry at least one paid request as the circuit-budget check computes it, and project relays enforce per-provider reservation, circuit and bandwidth quotas. This change fixes no limit values: a project relay's values are implementation inputs measured by the release gates.
- Discovery of NAT-only providers fits relay budgets: serving-grade circuits must fit a full manifest exchange, searches bound their relayed fan-out per relay, and results say when relay limits made them incomplete.
- A transport failure is recovered through request status and the existing funding state machine, never a blind paid retry.
- The active path (Direct, Hole-punched, Relayed with the relay's peer ID and limits, or HTTPS) is disclosed before funding and during use. For Relayed, the disclosure says what the relay sees: both peers' IP addresses and peer IDs, timing and volume. For Direct and Hole-punched, it says that each side's public IP is revealed to the other. Qualification and waivers stay provider-side.
- **BREAKING (wire):** offer schema v2 adds a signed `transports` field (`https`, `libp2p`) and makes `endpoint` nullable (required exactly when `https` is declared), signed under the new domain `chiral-inference-offer-v2`. Per the author's 2026-11-16 decision, there is no acceptance window: v1 never shipped publicly, so v2 replaces it at once. v2 offers are served only on a new manifest protocol, `/chiral/inference/manifest/2`, so a stale node fails cleanly instead of misdecoding. Upgraded nodes retire `/1` and stop publishing, discovering, quoting and funding v1 offers, and private testnet nodes upgrade together. Retirement governs only new discovery, quotes and accounts: top-ups, requests and recovery of an existing account continue under its saved terms, and v1 offers, quotes and receipts retained in saved accounts and provider ledgers stay parseable and verifiable for the life of each account.
- **BREAKING (wire), free allowance (author decision 10, 2026-11-16):** a free v2 offer (both token rates and minimum funding zero) signs a per-account `free_allowance`: `tokens`, counting input and output tokens together (1,000–10,000,000), and `lifetime_secs` from the account's opening (3,600–2,592,000), with native defaults of 20,000 tokens and 86,400 seconds; a priced offer carries `null`. Quote schema v2 copies it, under the new quote domain `chiral-inference-quote-v2`, and the agreement binds it; v1 quotes stay verification-only for retained accounts. The provider records each free account's opening time, reserves the worst case (`P + G` tokens) against the allowance before dispatch, never lets settled usage exceed it, and refuses later requests with HTTP 402 `free_allowance_exhausted` or `free_allowance_expired`; the consumer shows the allowance before opening. Auto-price changes (milestone 4) are ordinary offer revisions with no other protocol change, and existing accounts keep their terms. Requests on free accounts, including milestone 5's probes, pass the same circuit-budget check as paid requests, and relay grading stays blind to price and allowance.
- Explicit provider, consumer and relay configuration blocks, all strict JSON, and operator provisioning and preflight for libp2p-only deployments.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `p2p-provider-transport`: the libp2p serving stream, its one-router parity with the public listener and its stream-server bounds; consumer peer-ID authentication and stream-client rules; Kademlia mode and AutoNAT on every node; provider reachability; the connectivity ladder; address filtering; the v2 manifest protocol and the retirement of `/1`; the discovery relay budget; serving-relay limits and per-circuit serving-grade admission with its 24-hour grace; circuit-budget checks before paid and free-account dispatch; transport-failure recovery; path disclosure; and provider, consumer and operator-provisioning configuration.

### Modified Capabilities

- `inference-commercial-terms` (pending in `specify-inference-commercial-terms`, not yet in `openspec/specs/`): *Complete signed offer declaration* gains schema v2 with `transports` and a nullable `endpoint`, structural v1/v2 discrimination, the retirement of v1 publication, and a revision stream holding only v2 offers that continues the provider's one counter. *Offer freshness and advertised origin* refuses v1 offers for every fresh use, with no acceptance window, keeps retained v1 agreements verifiable and serviceable for the life of each account, and limits the HTTPS origin rules to offers that declare `https`. *Complete signed offer declaration* also gains the free allowance, required exactly on free offers, and treats a price change as an ordinary revision. *Complete signed quote and immutable copied terms* gains quote schema v2, which copies the allowance. A new requirement, *Free accounts are served only within their signed allowance*, enforces it. *Authenticated opening admission preserves original acceptance* stops counting an ended free account (allowance lifetime passed or tokens used, nothing unsettled) toward the 10,000-account ledger limit for free openings, while the per-wallet limit of eight still counts it (book *Wire change*, 2026-11-16). The allowance requirement also classifies a provider's HTTP 503 `qualification_in_progress` for native consumers as retryable and not admitted. *Native quote verification binds the chosen provider and payer* requires a v2 offer and quote for new onboarding, with an exactly equal allowance, and validates the origin or the authenticated peer ID, depending on the transport that returned the quote. **Archive-order dependency:** `specify-inference-commercial-terms` must be archived before this change, and these deltas must be rebased if its text changes first.
- `inference-wire-foundation`: *Length-framed operation domains* adds the `chiral-inference-offer-v2` domain, binds each offer schema to its own domain, and keeps `chiral-inference-offer-v1` only for verifying retained offers. *Distinct offer identities and payment commitments* computes an offer ID under its own schema's domain, so existing v1 offer IDs and the quote commitments built on them stay unchanged. The domain table also adds `chiral-inference-quote-v2`, keeps `chiral-inference-quote-v1` for verifying retained quotes, and computes each opening commitment under its quote's own domain.
