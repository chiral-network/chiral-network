## 2. Implement

- [ ] 2.1 Offer schema v2 in `protocol.rs`, `offer_registry.rs`, `contracts.rs` and `consumer.rs`: the `transports` field, an `endpoint` that is always present and nullable, the `chiral-inference-offer-v2` domain, structural v1/v2 decoding, an offer ID computed under each schema's own domain, and a revision stream holding only v2 offers with equivocation detection. Retire v1 with no acceptance window (design.md decision 5): no new signature under the v1 domain; v1 offers refused, with a retired-schema reason, for discovery, registry insertion, catalog listing, selection, quote proposal and acceptance; the provider refusing funding-intent registration or free opening for a quote it issued against a v1 offer without a durable acknowledgement or account. Keep retained v1 offers, quotes, acknowledgements and receipts in saved accounts and provider ledgers parseable and verifiable for recovery, requests, status, receipts, cancellation, top-ups and settlement.
- [ ] 2.2 Manifest protocol in `dht.rs`: `/chiral/inference/manifest/2` with `/1`'s framing and bounds; `/1` neither served nor requested; a v1 payload on a `/2` page treated as an invalid manifest; withdrawal by complete `/2` manifest; peers without `/2` treated as having no offers, withdrawing none and not marking the result incomplete. The relay catalog lists only v2 offers, and consumers decode catalog entries one by one, skipping v1 entries.
- [ ] 2.3 Provider configuration and publication in `provider_config.rs` and `app.rs`: a nullable `public_endpoint`; a strict `libp2p_serving` block with mandatory nonzero stream-server limits; `transports` derived from configuration; rejection when neither transport is configured; v2-only publication on the persisted DHT identity, continuing the one persisted revision counter; no v1 publication.
- [ ] 2.4 Provider stream listener: build the public router once and serve clones of it on both listeners; serve inbound `/chiral/inference/http/1` yamux streams; ignore `Host` and peer ID as authority; key source controls on the peer ID; enforce total and per-peer stream caps, the request-head deadline, the maximum head size, the idle timeout and the maximum requests per stream before the router's limits; refuse serving streams on coordination-sized inbound circuits; give a stream reset the same handling as an HTTP disconnect.
- [ ] 2.5 Swarm reachability in `dht.rs` for every node role: AutoNAT, with restricted, rate-bounded dial-back answers; Kademlia client/server mode by confirmed reachability, replacing forced server mode; relay client and DCUtR; reservation renewal and re-reservation with bounded backoff, independent of offers; publication of confirmed direct and relayed addresses; AutoNAT classification, Kademlia mode, reservation limits and per-relay circuit grade in `/admin/status`.
- [ ] 2.6 Learned-address filter using `offer_registry.rs::public_ip` for identify, Kademlia, DCUtR, AutoNAT dial-back and relayed addresses: learned DNS addresses not dialed, at most eight per peer, configured addresses exempt, development limited to loopback.
- [ ] 2.7 Serving-relay mode for `relay_server` and `chiral_daemon`: a strict `serving_relay` block with mandatory nonzero limits, including reservation and circuit rate limits and the discovery byte floor; coordination-sized reservations and circuits for any peer; serving-grade limits decided per circuit from the destination's own `/2` manifest, or within the grace that ends 24 hours after the signed `expires_at` of the most recent qualifying offer the relay verified, compared on the relay's wall clock in Unix seconds with no skew allowance, ended at the latest when the wall clock reaches the recorded wall-clock verification time plus 90,030 seconds and, within one process lifetime, 90,030 seconds after verification on its monotonic clock (design.md decision 12); durable offer-evidence records, one per peer within the relay's network, holding the most recently verified offer's content ID, `expires_at`, wall-clock verification time (persisted) and monotonic verification time (in memory) (the latest `expires_at` when one response holds several qualifying offers), kept in a protected, owner-only, network-scoped store under the relay's data directory, durably written before a serving-grade decision relies on a new or replaced record, reloaded on start with the clock rule reapplied, removed when their grace end passes and capped by the mandatory relay-wide `max_offer_evidence_records` (at least `max_reservations`) with earliest-grace-end eviction (ties: oldest verification); fail-closed grace for a missing, unreadable, corrupt or insecure store or a failed write (no reloaded records, records discarded on a failed write, in-memory records without grace until the store is sound, so serving-grade only for a current verified offer, no overwrite or deletion of a damaged store, no invented records); a loopback-only, bearer-authenticated administration status on `relay_server` reporting store condition, record count and maximum, eviction count and last eviction time; evidence refresh when a reserved destination's recorded offer expires, at most once per 120 seconds per destination with one request in flight, leaving the record unchanged when no qualifying offer is found; the grace kept across a restart; grace circuits counted against the same per-peer limits; open circuits keeping their limits after offers lapse or the grace ends; whole-circuit duration and two-way byte accounting; per-peer circuit and bandwidth limits counting circuits where the peer is the source or the destination (the per-provider quotas); serving-grade decisions independent of the offer's prices and serving profile; limits reported in reservation and connect replies; non-serving nodes limited to coordination size.
- [ ] 2.8 Consumer transport in `consumer.rs`: offer-driven transport selection preferring HTTPS when both are declared; peer-ID equality before any bytes are written; the bounded dial ladder with a ten-second deadline per connection attempt; the relay allowlist in a strict consumer `p2p` block; no redirect following, no dialing of response-named addresses, and 3xx surfaced without `Location`; the HTTPS client's response deadlines, idle timeout and 8 MiB bound; whole-circuit budget tracking and the pre-dispatch circuit-budget check with DCUtR or fresh-circuit fallback; no serving streams on coordination-sized circuits; per-request path classification; libp2p-only offers shown as unreachable from a profile without a `dht` node.
