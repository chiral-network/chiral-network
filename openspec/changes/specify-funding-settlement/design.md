## Decisions

1. **Specify one policy at its whitepaper name, with bounded freshness.** `canonical-successful-receipt-at-espace-safe-v1` is the contract. The book says head and safe-frontier freshness "are checked" (line 514). The requirement therefore states finite configured freshness limits and requires that a stale `safe` frontier alone makes the view unhealthy. The current values are listed in decision 6.
2. **Verification and monitoring classify contradictory chain evidence as unavailable, not as a payment rejection.** Book line 514 groups contradictory with unavailable evidence ("unavailable/contradictory evidence, **not proof of a reorg**") and says outages withhold "unverifiable new credit". Verification therefore has four outcomes: accepted, pending, rejected, unavailable. *Rejected* applies only when the signed bytes contradict the agreement, or when the payment's own execution failed canonically at `safe`. A configured-identity mismatch or a receipt that contradicts the signed transaction is untrustworthy chain evidence. It gives `settlement_unavailable` and is never attributed to the payment. Monitoring has five outcomes: accepted, pending, orphaned, unavailable and contradictory. *Contradictory* retains the last observed funding state, like an outage, and additionally closes new admission. Only a stable replacement of the accepted block proves an orphan. A receipt above `safe` after safe regression is pending, which withholds spending but reverses nothing. Author question 2 asks the author to confirm that contradictory evidence leaves already-accepted credit spendable.
3. **Loss is the current unfunded liability.** The ledger recomputes `available` and `provider_loss` from the totals instead of accumulating debits, so the order of restorations cannot create credit (`billing_store::balance_funding`). Sealed or remitted fee batches are never rewritten. Their liability stays, and any shortfall becomes loss.
4. **Top-up eligibility stays with the normative top-up contract.** `inference-account-topup-request` refuses top-up settlement for every non-active account. Requirement 10 cites it rather than restating it. Author question 1 covers the case it leaves open.
5. **Resume and cancellation are distinct paths, and cancellation blocks only its own operation.** Book line 516 says the two paths are distinct, and line 518 says a displaced payment that becomes canonical again is reconciled without a second credit or an automatic transfer. A cancellation request therefore blocks only rebroadcast of, and new payment for, the cancelled operation. It does not block account refresh or recovery of another pending operation. When any reconciliation, including the read-only `funding-recovery`, observes `original_accepted`, the consumer persists that outcome and `resume` settles the original. Recovering a won original never requires cancellation authorization or a call that could resubmit the replacement. The cancellation command may also settle a won original, as it does today, but it is not the only path. The current code differs on both points (code differences 6 and 7).
6. **Current implementation values (not normative constants).** Each may change without a spec change if the requirement still holds:
   - Head within 120 seconds of the local clock (`MAX_HEAD_AGE_SECONDS`); `safe` frontier no older than 300 seconds (`MAX_SAFE_AGE_SECONDS`); `eth_syncing` must be exactly `false`.
   - The 8 MiB block limit is selected when `eth_getBlockByNumber` has exactly two parameters and the second is `false` (`rpc_client::response_limit`).
   - Monitoring runs nominally every 30 seconds and at provider recovery, before offers are published (`app.rs` refresh). Each pass reads up to 100 payments ordered by `checked` time, then row ID (`BillingStore::funding_to_recheck`).
   - The cancellation replacement is a legacy EIP-155 transaction with a 21,000 gas limit, and the node's estimate must equal 21,000. Native originals are always legacy (`chain_client::sign_transaction` builds a nine-field EIP-155 envelope), so `P` is the legacy gas price. The decoder uses the maximum fee per gas if a typed original is ever journaled.
   - The account identity check uses 256-bit integers (`U256` in `billing_store::save_account`).
   - Orphaned fee obligations use the statuses `cancelled`, `orphaned_batched` and `orphaned_remitted`. Restoration returns them to `pending`, `batched` or `remitted`.
   - An unprepared cancellation reports status `not_prepared`.

## Code differences

Each difference has an unchecked task in section 2 of `tasks.md`.

1. **Desktop residual-risk disclosure** (requirement 2, book line 489). `src/pages/Marketplace.svelte` line 230 says "The provider must acknowledge the signed funding intent before payment is broadcast". Line 240 says "payment is broadcast only after the provider's signed acknowledgement". Both imply that the acknowledgement prevents broadcast. Neither discloses provider-held broadcastable bytes, that the ordering is not escrow, or that an error does not prove no money moved. Task 2.1.
2. **CLI `fund` disclosure is incomplete** (requirement 2, book line 489). `bin/chiral.rs` line 226 says the provider "could broadcast them without acknowledging" and "On pending/error inspect pending and resume the contract". It does not say "before acknowledging", does not say that the ordering is not cryptographic escrow, and does not say that an error, timeout or missing reply does not prove no money moved. Task 2.2.
3. **Desktop top-up reorganization disclosure** (requirement 3, book lines 813 and 929). The Usage top-up card (`Usage.svelte` line 207) says only "reorg risk remains". It does not say that reorganizations can freeze or reverse credit, and its acceptance control (line 212) accepts only a "provider-specific non-refundable top-up". Task 2.3.
4. **Contradictory chain evidence is attributed to the payment or withholds accepted credit** (requirements 1, 5, 9 and 12, book line 514). `ChainClient::identity_check` returns `Rejected` for a chain-ID or genesis mismatch (chain_client.rs:561–575), and `execution_receipt_fields` returns `Rejected` for a receipt whose hash, sender, recipient, type or gas contradicts the signed transaction (chain_client.rs:658–690).
   - In new verification, `contracts::verify_funding` maps `Rejected` to 400 `funding_rejected` (contracts.rs:559). It records the attempt as `rejected`, and `record_funding_attempt` then zeroes the acknowledged intent's `admission_until` (billing_store.rs:810). It does not close `funding_ready` (contracts.rs:554 closes it only for `Unavailable`).
   - In monitoring, `apply_funding_recheck` maps every `Err(Rejected)` to `pending` (billing_store.rs:1334–1337). An identity mismatch therefore moves every accepted payment it reaches to `settlement_pending` and withholds spending.
   - Task 2.4.
5. **One failing payment stalls monitoring** (requirement 9). `contracts::reconcile` applies each observation with `?` (contracts.rs:604–608). A ledger refusal in `transition_funding` aborts the whole pass without advancing that row's `checked` time (billing_store.rs:1360–1365). One example is the refusal of a refreshed inclusion that changes immutable evidence. Every later pass then starts and stops at the same oldest row, so other credited payments are never rechecked. The provider does fail closed: the refresh error withdraws offers (app.rs:315–320). Task 2.5.
6. **A won original is recoverable only through the cancellation command** (requirements 16 and 17, book lines 516 and 518). `resume_inner` refuses whenever `funding_cancellation_requested` is set unless the cached `saved.funding_cancellation.status` is `original_accepted` (consumer.rs:1070–1080). Only `cancel_funding` writes that field (consumer.rs:1014). `Consumer::funding_recovery` reconciles the journal (consumer.rs:1038–1069) but never updates the saved outcome, and `ChainClient::cancellation_status` (chain_client.rs:1214) has no caller. For a top-up reported `cancelled`, the saved pending top-up is cleared, so a later win is reachable only through `cancel-funding --operation-id`. The user must pass `--accept-gas-and-race-risk` to recover an accepted original. While the original's nonce is still current, that same call resubmits the replacement (chain_client.rs:1259–1330). Task 2.6.
7. **An opening cancellation blocks every later resume of the agreement** (requirements 4 and 16, book line 493 and A.1.7). `cancel_funding` sets the contract-wide `funding_cancellation_requested` for any `open:` cancellation, including on a saved account (consumer.rs:962). `resume_inner` checks it before the top-up branch (consumer.rs:1072–1081), and `fund` calls `resume_inner` (consumer.rs:900). Unless the outcome is `original_accepted`, `resume` and `fund` can no longer refresh the saved account or recover a later pending top-up. The desktop offers only `resume` for a pending top-up, and its top-up form is disabled while pending (`Usage.svelte` lines 41 and 210–217). `topup` with the same amount bypasses the gate. Any fix must also skip the opening payment step for a saved account whose opening was cancelled, because `prepare_payment_once` refuses a `cancelled` journal record. Task 2.7.
8. **A top-up cancellation request is not durable before the cancellation attempt** (requirement 16, book line 516). For an opening, `cancel_funding` saves `funding_cancellation_requested` before it calls `cancel_payment_once`. For a pending top-up it saves only the original bytes (consumer.rs:1044–1067). If `cancel_payment_once` then fails before it journals a replacement, for example because of insufficient cancellation gas, an RPC outage or an estimate mismatch, nothing records the request. The pending top-up intent remains, and `resume_topup` calls `send_payment_once` (consumer.rs:1539). The chain-level refusal applies only when a cancellation record exists (chain_client.rs:1573), so while the original's nonce is still current, `resume` can broadcast an original whose cancellation was requested. Task 2.8.
9. **The desktop does not re-read the funding journal after a top-up or resume attempt** (requirement 2, book lines 489, 814 and 929). `Usage.svelte` `topup()` and `resume()` (lines 37–41) update the top-up card's `pending` flag but do not reload `pending_accounts`. After a top-up error, timeout or lost reply, the *Funding & recovery* tab still says no funding is pending and the header's journaled-operation notice does not appear, so the journaled stage is not shown until the user presses *Refresh*. After a successful resume, the stale notice can remain. `select()` recomputes `pending` from the stale journal, so switching accounts and back can re-enable the top-up form; native `topup` still resumes the same intent, so no second payment results. Task 2.9.

No other difference from the whitepaper was found. The policy, orphan and restoration arithmetic, cancellation price and resubmission, targeting, journal sharing and nonce ownership match the requirements.

## Observations outside this capability

- An existing **frozen** account answers opening replay with `account_frozen` instead of returning the retained account (`BillingStore::open_account`). A consumer whose original opening reply was lost cannot save the account locally until restoration. Authorized reads remain available. This boundary belongs to `inference-account-opening-request` and `inference-account-snapshot` and is not changed here.
- The normative top-up recovery clause ("retaining pending local evidence until successful acceptance is confirmed and persisted") has no native test for an `account_frozen` refusal of a submitted pending top-up. That evidence gap belongs to `inference-account-topup-request`.
- The Usage top-up card also omits other A.1.6 items: that the provider sees prompts in plaintext, that signed metadata does not prove execution, and the fee rate. Only the reorganization item belongs to this capability.

## Open questions for the whitepaper author

1. **Top-ups to non-active accounts.** The normative top-up contract refuses settlement for every non-active account: `frozen` (an orphaned opening) and the transient `settlement_pending`. Consider a top-up whose payment already executed before its account became `frozen`. If the opening's orphan is permanent, the top-up can never be credited, and the provider keeps on-chain funds that never become credit. That conflicts with book line 495 ("readiness gates new admission, not recovery of a previously funded immutable agreement") and with the `CLAUDE.md` rule that historical funding recovery stays reachable without abandoning accepted balances. Should such a top-up be credited despite the freeze, held until restoration, or be allowed to strand? The original stays journaled and resumable on the consumer meanwhile, so this change records no code difference for it.
2. **Contradictory evidence and accepted credit.** Requirement 9 keeps already-accepted credit spendable when the configured RPC fails its identity check or returns a receipt that contradicts the signed transaction. It treats this like an outage, following line 514's "unavailable/contradictory" grouping, and closes new admission. The current code instead withholds spending (`settlement_pending`). Should contradictory evidence leave accepted credit spendable, or withhold it without reversal?
3. **CLI reorganization disclosure.** A.1.6 requires the desktop to show and obtain acceptance of "reorganizations can freeze or reverse credit" before funding. The book states no equivalent for the CLI `fund` and `topup` commands, so requirement 3 is desktop-only. Should the CLI carry it too?

## Other notes

- `funding-recovery` reconciles before listing, so it fails while the RPC is unavailable. The local `pending` and `history` views stay available offline. The book does not require offline reconciliation.

## Risks

- Every executable evidence above is synthetic: loopback JSON-RPC fixtures and ledger transitions. Live eSpace reorg, safe-frontier regression, cancellation races and fee-batch orphaning have not been observed on testnet chain 71 for this protocol. That remains a public-testnet release gate in `docs/llm-serving-migration-plan.md`, tracked here as task 4.1.
- The implementation values in decision 6 are not normative. Changing one needs no spec change, but this design should be updated to match.
- Requirements reference capabilities from pending changes (task 4.3 sets the archive order).
